05 The field

What clinics run on today

80%+

Paper

Patient cards, exercise-book registers, carbon receipt books. Cheap, familiar, and impossible to search, total up or report from.

Some

Excel & notebooks

A spreadsheet for revenue, another for patients. Breaks the moment two people need it at once, and nobody can say which copy is current.

Few

Imported international software

Licensed from abroad and priced in dollars. Gregorian-only dates, support in a different time zone, and features built for a market that isn't this one.

Few

One-off custom builds

Commissioned once from a developer who has since moved on. Works until it doesn't — no updates, no owner, no way back.

Rare

Hospital-grade HMIS

Built for hospitals with an IT department. Too heavy, too expensive and too slow to deploy for a clinic with six staff.

Categories, not named vendors — send me the specific products you want called out and I'll name them.

10 Marketing strategy

We don't ask clinics to buy. We ask them to help.

A clinic owner has a ready answer for a salesperson. They have no answer prepared for "we've built something for clinics like yours — would you tell us what you think of it?" That one change of question is the whole strategy.

Ask for feedback→ Build the room→ Let them ask us
1 Get in the door

Feedback, not a pitch

Interns & commission-only reps
  • Each rep visits 15–50 clinics in person
  • They ask for an opinion on the platform — they never ask for a sale
  • They collect contacts: the owner, the doctors, the nurses
  • No pressure means no rejection, so the door stays open
Cost: one-time payment per rep — no salaries, no ad spend
2 Build the room

A Telegram group of clinics

From ~50 clinics onward
  • Every contact collected joins the channel and group
  • We demo the platform there and ask what they'd change
  • They are advisors, not leads — and advisors get invested
  • A clinic that helped shape it asks to use it, unprompted
An owned audience — no algorithm, no ad budget
3 Turn on content

Clips first, campaigns later

Once the room is full
  • Short clips captured during the demos we're already doing
  • Posted as Telegram stories and into the group
  • Only then a dedicated push on Facebook and TikTok
  • Content is a by-product of phase 2, not a production line
Nothing is filmed until there's an audience to film for
By the time we mention price, they've been using the product for weeks.

This inverts the usual cost curve: no advertising spend until we already have the room, and no video production until the demos are producing the footage for free.
