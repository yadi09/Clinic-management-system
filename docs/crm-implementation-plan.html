ClinicOS CRM — implementation plan

Leads, campaigns and phases · 25 September 2026

The prototype's simplicity and ClinicOS's campaign model are not in conflict — one is the default case of the other. A lead's visible stage is simply its phase in whichever campaign it is currently in. Seed one house campaign and day one looks exactly like the prototype; create a second and you have something the prototype could never do. Everything below follows from that.

01The shape of it

Four objects. Everything else is a view over them.

LEAD (a dental clinic we want as a customer) ├ lifecycle open · won · lost · dormant ← the whole relationship ├ score 0–100, computed ├ owner the rep responsible ├ priority hot · high · medium · low │ ├ ENROLMENT ──→ CAMPAIGN ──→ PHASES (ordered) │ one active "Addis dental New → Contacted → Interested │ + history blitz Q4" → Demo → Trial → Negotiation │ → Won / Lost │ └ current phase ← THIS is the "Stage" a rep sees on the lead │ └ phase events ← who moved it, when, and the note │ ├ TASKS follow-ups with a due date and a snooze └ ACTIVITIES calls, WhatsApp, demos, meetings, notes, and stage changes

Acting on one

  • Log a touch — call, WhatsApp, demo, meeting, note. One click from the row.
  • Move to the next phase, with a note recorded against the move.
  • Schedule a follow-up — or let it be created automatically (see §04).
  • Reassign to another rep; reprioritise; enrol in another campaign.
  • Close as won → links to the clinic's workspace; or lost → with a reason.

03Campaigns and phases

What you asked for specifically: a campaign per push, with phases leads must pass.

Creating a campaign

  1. Name and goal — "Enkutatash 2019 dental push", win 20 clinics before Meskel.
  2. Phases, in order. Each has a name, an intent (what has to be true to leave it) and an expected duration — the last being new, and the thing that turns a phase from a label into a commitment.
  3. Enrol leads — by filter ("all Hot in Bole"), by tick-box, or by rule ("every new lead from the sign-up form").

A phase is defined by

name            "Demo Scheduled"
intent          "A date is agreed with the decision-maker"
expected_days   5          ← breach after this flags the lead as stalled
entry_rule      optional   e.g. requires contact_name to be set

How a lead moves

enrol ──→ phase 1 ──→ phase 2 ──→ … ──→ won │ │ │ └─ every move writes a phase_event: │ who moved it, when, and why └─ sits too long → STALLED (expected_days breached)

Moving is the only way the board changes, and every move is recorded. That gives the funnel drop-off report for free, because the data is the same data.

Multiple campaigns on one lead

Permitted — there is no constraint preventing it, and it is genuinely useful: a clinic that went cold on cold-outreach can be enrolled in the Enkutatash push without losing its history. But one enrolment is marked active, and that is the one whose phase shows as the lead's stage. Otherwise "what stage is this clinic at?" has no answer.

On the eleven stages. The prototype's ladder — New, Researched, Contacted, Responded, Interested, Demo Scheduled, Demo Completed, Trial, Negotiation, Won, Lost — is well thought out but long. Four reps will not keep eleven columns honest; in practice Researched and Responded get skipped and the board lies. I would seed the house campaign with seven: New → Contacted → Interested → Demo → Trial → Negotiation → Won/Lost and let anyone who wants the longer ladder build it as their own campaign. Being able to disagree with me on this, per campaign, is exactly the point of the model.

04Improvements on both

Neither system has these. Ordered by what I think they are worth.

1 · The conversion loop only ClinicOS can do this

A won prospect already carries tenant_id — the workspace it became. So the CRM can show what no standalone CRM ever can:

Won 12 Sept  →  workspace "bole-dental"  →  registered 41 patients,
                 3 staff signed in, last activity 2 days ago

Which turns "we closed it" into "they are actually using it", and makes churn visible before the renewal conversation rather than at it. It also feeds the campaign: a clinic that won a free implementation and never logged in needs a phone call.

2 · Stalled, not just overdue

The prototype flags leads whose task is overdue. The more dangerous lead is the one with no task at all — sitting in "Demo Scheduled" for 21 days, invisible, because nobody ever set a reminder. Phase age against the phase's expected duration catches those. This is the single biggest gap in the prototype's logic.

3 · Loss reasons

The prototype has "Lost". It does not ask why. Capture a reason — price · chose a competitor · not ready · no response · closed down — and within a month you know whether you have a pricing problem or a product problem. Given the conversation we have been having about plan pricing, this is the field that would settle it with evidence instead of instinct.

4 · Next best action

The score already knows what is missing. Turn that into an instruction on the row:

Score 55  ·  Next: get the doctor's name  (+15)

Same data, but it tells a rep what to do rather than how they are being judged.

5 · One-tap WhatsApp and Telegram

Ethiopia runs on both, and the prototype scores WhatsApp but does not use it. A link that opens the chat with an appropriate opening message, and logs the touch when it is used — that is minutes saved on every lead, every day.

6 · Activity creates the next task

Log "demo held" and the follow-up is created three days out automatically. The prototype's 68 overdue follow-ups exist partly because creating them is a separate act of discipline. Make the discipline automatic and the queue stays honest.

7 · Territory by subcity

Assign a rep to a set of subcities and let new leads route themselves. With 33 named subcities and four reps, this removes a weekly admin job and stops two reps calling the same clinic on the same day.

8 · Merge duplicates, not just detect them

ClinicOS finds duplicates; it cannot yet combine them. Four reps scraping Google Maps will create them constantly, and a duplicate you cannot merge is a duplicate you keep.

Scoring

score = min(100,
    15×(valid Ethiopian mobile)   + 15×(contact name AND role)
  + 10×(whatsapp set)             + 10×(rating > 4.0)
  + 15×(chairs >= 3)
  + highest_stage_bonus)          -- 25 demo · 30 trial · 35 negotiation

Note the last line. The prototype adds the stage bonuses together, so anything in negotiation is pinned at 100 and the score stops sorting exactly where it matters most. Take the highest reached, not the sum.

07Not doing

  • Not importing the prototype's 100 fabricated clinics — they would pollute a pipeline that holds 7 real prospects.
  • Not rebuilding auth, invites or the RBAC matrix — ClinicOS is far ahead; the prototype prints its password on the login page.
  • Not building Clear Database or Reload Sample Data — coherent in a toy, a loaded gun on real data.
  • Not starting before backups exist. Seven new columns and a new table on a database with no backup increases what there is to lose.

08Decide before phase 1

  1. Phases in the house campaign — my seven, or the prototype's eleven?
  2. Where it lives — inside the platform console, or its own app at /crm? If reps who are not vendor admins will use it, it needs to be its own app with its own login.
  3. How far — all seven phases, or stop after three and watch whether the reps use it?
  4. The sign-up feed — should Enkutatash registrations become leads automatically, or wait for someone to approve each one?
