ClinicOS · design proposal · awaiting approval

The visit panel does
four jobs at once

It is the first thing on the patient page and the hardest thing to read. The cause is not decoration — the panel renders every one of its parts in every situation, including the parts that contradict each other.

The bug underneath the crowding

Before any design question: the panel in your screenshot is describing a visit booked for 22 August — eleven days away, and it says “Visit recorded.” Directly beneath, the same visit is labelled “Not yet due.”

SettleFee renders whenever a session is not paid, not queued and not flagged pay-later. It never checks whether the visit happened. So the product asserts a clinical event that has not occurred and offers two buttons to bill for it — the exact failure the money work spent a day removing everywhere else, reappearing in the newest surface.

That single fault explains most of what makes the panel feel crowded: an entire tinted band, the loudest element on screen, should not be there at all.

What the audit found

criticalThe panel tells you a visit happened when it has not

SettleFee renders only when session.status === 'done'.

The amber band says the 4,000 is unsettled and demands a decision. The fee line directly beneath it reads "Not yet due". Both are generated from the same session. A user cannot reconcile them, and the correct one is the quiet one.
One money statement per state. Before the visit: the fee on the work line, nothing else. After: the settle band, nothing else.
highThe primary action is a lie on most visits
"Record today's work" is rendered for any planned session, including one eleven days out. Pressing it opens a form that will stamp a visit that has not happened.
Label the action for the state: "Start this visit now" for a future visit, with the date consequence stated; "Record today's work" only when it is actually today.
highEffect is printed above cause
Reading top to bottom: the money consequence of recording a visit, then a link to correct the visit, then what happened last time, then the button that records the visit. The panel is in reverse causal order, which is why it reads as a pile rather than a sequence.

Five bordered or tinted regions stack with no hierarchy. The largest and most saturated is the amber money band, which in this state should not be on screen at all. A first-time user's eye lands on the one thing that does not apply.

The visit fee once, on the work line. The course total once, under the rail. The settle band names the amount only when it is asking a question about it.
mediumRare repairs occupy prime vertical space
'Correct this visit' sits between the money band and the reader; 'Finish course' floats alone on its own row; '+ visit' is an unlabelled chip on the rail. Three rarely-used controls interrupt the everyday path.

The proposal: one panel, three states

The panel currently renders as though every visit is in every state at once. A visit is only ever in one of three, and each has exactly one thing you would want to do.

Rule 1. One filled primary button per state. If two things look equally important, neither is.
Rule 2. One money statement per state — never two, never in two vocabularies.
Rule 3. One tinted band at most, reserved for a real obligation that exists right now.
Rule 4. Rare repairs live in a ⋯ menu. Frequency decides prominence.
Rule 5. Reading order follows causal order: when → which course → what happened last → do the work → what it costs.

“Also open”

Today the panel ends with:

    ALSO OPEN
    treatment Two   session 3 of 6 · Sep 25, 2026 · in 45 days              ›
    Root Canal      session 2 of 6 · Sep 25, 2026 · in 45 days              ›

Both are 45 days away. Nothing is open. It reads as a to-do list and is a table of
contents — and the Timeline panel already lists them, in order, on the same page.

Proposed: this appears ONLY when another course needs attention today, and says why.

    ⚠ Also needs attention
      Root Canal · visit 2 was due 5 days ago                    [ switch to it ]

Otherwise: nothing. Silence is information.

What this does not change

The resolver, the money model, the safety strip and the shelf all stay exactly as they are. This is a rewrite of one component's layout and render conditions — roughly VisitCockpit.tsx and the placement of SettleFee. No backend change, no migration, no change to what anything means.

The one behavioural change is the bug fix: a visit that has not happened will no longer offer to be billed.
