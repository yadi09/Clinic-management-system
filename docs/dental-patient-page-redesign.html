ClinicOS · Design study

The dental patient page,
rebuilt around the visit

Five independent audits of the running code, three competing redesigns, three adversarial judges, one plan. Commissioned because the page “has a lot of issues” — it does, and they are worse than they look.

The verdict

Direction B — "The Chair"

with the timeline from A and the money-by-state grouping, URL state and dialog-level safety line from C grafted in. The page becomes: PATIENT BAR → SAFETY STRIP → THE COCKPIT (resolves to the one visit that is happening, and is where work gets recorded) → THE RECORD SHELF (one surface, one panel open at a time, replacing six stacked cards). Reception gets the same skeleton with a different action slot and a different shelf, fed by a projection type that has no clinical fields on it.

The clinic owner's complaint is "recurring medicals". Every day, roughly 25 times, a dentist opens a patient who is mid-course and needs to answer three questions — where are we, what did I do last time, what do I do now — and then write one paragraph. Today that costs ten-plus interactions, two typed justifications, and a guess about which filter to change. The Chair costs three clicks and no typing that isn't clinical, because the top of the page *resolves* to the visit instead of *filtering* to it.

Concretely, for Cure Dental:

- The page never again opens on last year's finished scaling. `focusVisit()` walks today → overdue → next → last done → complete → first visit and always names the rung it landed on ("OVERDUE · was Thu 7 Aug · 3 days ago"). There is no date scope to reset and no tab counter that can read "In progress 0" for a patient with two live courses.

- The dentist finally sees "session 4 of 6" — the string reception, the appointments board and the schedule page have always shown and the clinical page never did.

- "What did I do last time" is on screen before anyone clicks, as read-only prose, and the reading surface is no longer the writing surface with a Save button next to it.

- Nobody has to send money to the cashier before they are allowed to write a clinical note. Treat → record → bill, which is the order it happens in the chair.

- A root canal that overruns can gain a seventh visit; one that seals in five can be closed. Both are things the product currently has no verb for.

- The front desk stops asking patients for money they have already queued to pay, because "At cashier" is subtracted from "Owed now" rather than shown beside it.

I picked B over C (the tab direction) because the two judges who counted actual work — everyday speed and implementation cost — both put B first, and C's own strongest argument (it kills ReceptionDentalView) is also its worst property: there is no half-tabbed page, so it cannot be shipped in increments against a live clinic. B's stages (1) and (2) change no layout at all and still fix real bugs; if the project were stopped after them, Cure Dental would be strictly better off. That matters more than elegance when the customer is treating patients on this code today.

I picked B over A (the pure timeline) because dentistry asks a set question — "which of her three courses is which and how much is left on each" — and a date-sorted spine scatters it. A's own author says so. So I took A's timeline as the *history* surface, where it is unbeatable, and kept B's cockpit as the *action* surface.

The worst moment in each lens

Each reviewer was asked to name the single worst moment in the current experience, as a concrete scenario.

FRONT DESK / RECEPTION + MONEY

A patient walks in for today's session of an ongoing root canal — the single most common event at Cure Dental's desk. The receptionist opens the patient, sees "Take payment", clicks it. The dialog opens with **nothing ticked and "0.00 ETB"**, because it defaults to `rows.filter((r) => moneyState(r.s) === "due")` and today's session is `status: "planned"` → `moneyState` returns `"scheduled"`, not `"due"` (ReceptionDentalView.tsx:426, :45-51). She now has to find today's session by eye in a flat, unsorted, ungrouped list of every unbilled session across every plan, where the only date shown is a raw ISO string (`{r.s.scheduled_date}`, line 455), no "Today" marker, no Ethiopian date, and the only badge is "Due" on rows that are *not* the one she wants. She ticks something, presses "Send to cashier", gets a toast — and no money has been collected, because reception with `payment.charge` cannot settle: there is no settle action anywhere on this page and no link to /cashier. Meanwhile the tile above her still reads "Due now" with the same amount she just sent, because `bal.due` counts billed sessions too. She asks the patient for money the patient is already queued to pay.

The recurring-treatment workflow

Patient sits down for session 4 of 6 of a root canal. The dentist scrolls ~1,000px past the tooth chart, the balance card, the medical-history card and every past examination to reach Treatment plans. The session row for today shows a circled "4" — not "4 of 6" — and the row above it says "1 of 6 sessions" (which actually means "showing 1 of 6") while the strip below says "3 of 6 sessions done". There is no sign of what happened at session 3; to read it the dentist must click the "All dates" scope, hunt the row, and press a button labelled **Record** to open session 3's *editable* form and read the notes out of a Textarea. Then, back on today's session, the "Record" button is greyed out: `title="Collect payment or mark pay-later to record this visit"`. The dentist cannot write down what they did until they have either sent 2,000 ETB to the cashier or typed a mandatory "promise to pay" note. Clinical documentation is held hostage by billing. And if the patient turns up three days late instead of on the day, none of this is even on screen — the page defaults to the "Today" scope, the root canal vanishes from both Today and Upcoming, and `autoTab` drops the dentist onto the **History** tab showing finished treatments.

Visual design, density & hierarchy

A patient returns for week 4 of a 6-session root canal — the exact recurring workflow the user says is broken. The dentist opens the page and the first fixation is a 30px bold number: the outstanding balance. `text-3xl font-bold tabular-nums` (DentalWorkspace.tsx:351) is the largest type anywhere on the page — larger than the patient's own name (`text-h2` = 21px, line 296) and larger than the design system's h1 token (26px). Nothing on the screen says "today". To reach today's session they scroll past seven visually identical white cards with identical 17px blue titles (`CardTitle` = `text-h3 font-bold text-brand` for all of them), find "Treatment plans", read a tab strip whose selected tab is a raised white chip (line 391), then a second control strip 12px to its right whose selected item is a solid blue pill (line 402) — two contradictory conventions for "selected" on one line — expand a plan whose header carries four badges at once (amber "In progress" meaning nothing is wrong, blue "Today" that only restates the filter already set, "Owes", and a tooth code, lines 505-511), read a four-cell money strip that repeats "Due now" at `text-sm` (line 538) — the same figure rendered at `text-3xl` two cards above — then a grey counter strip, then a date header, and finally a session row carrying a teal "Paid" pill beside an emerald percentage, two rose icon+text warnings and a grey image count. The action they came to perform — record the visit — is the last control in that row and the only `variant="ghost"` in it (line 713), visually quieter than "Collect" (`variant="outline"`, line 702) and quieter than the "Access now" lock button beside it (line 685).

Decisions that are yours, not mine

Question 1

1. REMOVING THE BILL-BEFORE-RECORD GATE IS A POLICY CHANGE, NOT ONLY A UX ONE — and it needs the clinic owner, not the dentist. Someone chose it deliberately, presumably because a session recorded and never billed is revenue lost. My call is to remove it (it is clinically backwards, it recurs on all six sessions of a course, and it is trivially satisfied by typing one character into the pay-later note, so it does not work as a control anyway). The replacement is: the unresolved fee persists as an amber line on the cockpit AND on the timeline row until settled, plus a clinic-wide "treatment given, never billed" report in phase 4. Please confirm with Cure Dental's owner before phase 3 ships, because if they say no, the honest alternative is a soft prompt on leaving the page rather than restoring the lock.

Question 2

2. WHO MAY WAIVE? "Free of charge" becomes a first-class, encouraged outcome in phase 4, and waiving is a real Addis workflow. Right now any dental.manage user could do it. My default is to keep it on dental.manage with a mandatory reason written to the charge, but if the owner wants it restricted to a manager that is a new permission (`payment.waive`) and needs a migration — cheap now, awkward later.

Question 3

3. CAN WE HAVE A TWO-RELEASE STAGGER? The plan deliberately cuts the dentist over in phase 3 and leaves the front desk on ReceptionDentalView.tsx until phase 6, so the two roles are never both in flux at the same time on a clinic that is treating patients today. That means roughly 3-4 weeks where the dentist's page and the desk's page look different from each other. Confirm that is acceptable; the alternative — one big cutover — is the single riskiest thing we could do to this customer.
