The visit card, redesigned

A design for the card at the top of a dental patient's page. Nothing in the app has changed yet. Each situation below is the same card, drawn the way it would look. The notes beside each one explain the choices.

What the card is for

  • Tell anyone, in one sentence, what is happening with this patient's treatment.
  • Show every visit with a word, not only a colour: done, missed, today, next, later.
  • Keep treatment progress and money apart, so neither is mistaken for the other.
  • Offer one obvious next step.

The only words it uses

  • Treatment: the whole plan, such as a root canal. Never "course", "session" or "plan".
  • Visit: one appointment in a treatment.
  • Done, Missed, Today, Next, Later: what has happened to a visit.
  • Paid, Left to pay, Paid ahead: the money.

What changed, and why
  1. Tabs name every treatment in progress, by teeth and start date, so three root canals are three different things. The red dot means "something is late here".
  2. The allergy is inside the card, right above the button you press to treat.
  3. One sentence says what's going on. It focuses on the next step of the treatment (visit 2), not the latest missed date, and names the other missed visit.
  4. Each visit has a word, a date, and its payment. Missed is red and says "Missed". Paid-but-not-done says "Paid ahead".
  5. Treatment and money are separate lines. "1 of 5 visits done" can't be mistaken for "89% paid".
  6. The main button says exactly what it does, and shows the date it will save. Today, unless you change it.

To decide before building

  1. Which treatment opens first when there are several?My suggestion: the one booked today; otherwise the one with the most recent missed or upcoming visit. The tabs make it a click to switch either way.
  2. Should "Record visit 2" default to today's date?My suggestion: yes. A missed visit done today happened today, and the records should say so. "Change date" covers entering yesterday's paper notes.
  3. What does "Rebook missed visits" do?My suggestion: one dialog that moves visits 2 and 3, and shifts the later ones along with them, so the plan stays in order.
  4. Three root canals on one patient: are these real, or duplicates from testing? If real clinics never have this, the tabs still help when a patient has, say, a root canal and braces at once.
