Pre-production audit: data integrity, money, isolation and recovery

Audit of ClinicOS at commit dee351d (deployed 2026-10-11 08:17 UTC), made on 2026-10-11 before more dental clinics are onboarded. It separates what was inspected, what was executed, what was observed in production, and what could not be verified. No code, data, configuration or infrastructure was changed by this audit.

Update, same day. The two pre-onboarding fixes are built and tested, and waiting for your "deploy": AUD-01 (editing a patient no longer erases passport, address or emergency contact, at both the screen and the server) and AUD-02 (deleting an image or attachment hides the record but keeps the stored file). Files still have no second copy (AUD-02 remains open for backup and retention). Everything else in this report is unchanged.
How this audit was kept safe. Production was touched only by read-only commands: server uptime, disk, container list, the backup directory listing and log, public-site requests, and counts over tables (no patient names, phones or records were read or copied). All writes were made against an isolated throwaway database that holds synthetic data only (about 3,800 test clinics). One scratch test file was created to probe a rounding rule and was deleted immediately; the working tree is unchanged.

1. Executive risk report

Recommendation: conditional GO for clinics on the dental preset; NO-GO for enabling pharmacy, inpatient, maternity or in-house laboratory on newly onboarded clinics. The conditions are at the end of this section. This is a recommendation for the owner to decide, not a guarantee.

What the evidence supports

  • Clinic isolation is sound at the database. Every table that holds a clinic's rows has row-level security enabled and forced with a policy (a test checks this for all of them and passes). I found no route that reads a clinic's data without a clinic context; the only direct database use outside the vendor console is a read of the clinic registry by slug.
  • Money records reconcile. On production (846 paid charges, 6 day-closes, 24 treatment plans) every invariant I tested holds with zero violations, except one historical anomaly that needs your confirmation (finding AUD-15). Money is stored as exact numeric; there is no floating-point money column in the schema.
  • Double payment is guarded and tested. Concurrent settles and concurrent refunds each take the money once (REG-PAY-005, 007); one bank transfer cannot pay twice (REG-BILL-032); overlapping appointments are refused by a database exclusion constraint.
  • Backups run. Nine consecutive nightly dumps (02:30 Addis), each verified readable, encrypted, and uploaded off the server to a separate bucket, plus a verified dump before every deploy.
  • Today's full run is green: 885 backend tests passed (0 failed), 1,841 frontend tests, 14 browser journeys, 277 of 277 in-scope routes exercised.

What the evidence does not support

  • That patient data cannot be lost. Editing a patient currently erases passport, address and emergency contact (AUD-01). That is active data loss in an everyday action.
  • That clinical files are safe. Deleting an X-ray destroys the file immediately and no copy of the file store exists (AUD-02). One image has already been deleted in production.
  • That a restore works. A restore of a production dump has not been demonstrated in this audit (it would move real patient data; see section 7). Recovery time is unknown.
  • That the general-clinic modules are safe. Pharmacy 1.8%, inpatient 0.2%, maternity 1.0%, clinical 33%, lab 39% statement coverage by the whole suite. They handle money and clinical data and are essentially unverified (AUD-09).
  • That anyone would be told when something breaks. There is no monitoring or missed-backup alert (AUD-07).

Conditions for onboarding tomorrow

  1. Fix or disable patient editing (AUD-01) before any new clinic registers patients. Small change, test already written. Needs your approval.
  2. Restrict deleting images to the owner and tell clinics not to delete; ask Cloudflare R2 to keep object versions (AUD-02). Needs your authorization for the bucket setting.
  3. Onboard on the dental preset only. The preset switches off laboratory, pharmacy, inventory, maternity and inpatient (provision.go); REG-PLAT-010 asserts dental on and laboratory, pharmacy and inventory off, and the other two are set in the same preset but not asserted by a test.
  4. Take a manual restore rehearsal to a throwaway server this week (AUD-07); until then, treat recovery as unproven.

Trust boundaries

  • Public internet → Cloudflare → origin. Port 8443 on the server is also reachable directly (verified), bypassing Cloudflare (AUD-16).
  • Signed-in staff → API: authorization (permission and clinic) is enforced on the server, tested by role matrices (REG-AUTHZ-001, -005).
  • Clinic → clinic: row-level security. Vendor console → all clinics: separate token, separate permission, separate table of admins (REG-PLAT-001..008).

An external port scan from the audit workstation reported every port "open"; the server's own socket list shows only 22, 80, 443 and 8443 listening, and neither Postgres, Redis nor the API is published. The scan result was a property of the workstation's network, not the server, and is not a finding.

Structural guarantees (database catalog of the test database)

  • Every one of 113 tables has a primary key. Money columns: 28 are numeric; zero are double precision, real or money.
  • Every timestamp is timestamptz (one exception: the migration tool's own table).
  • Foreign keys: 194 restrict deletion, 46 cascade, 4 set null. The cascades that matter: deleting a patient would cascade allergies, attachments, conditions, dental exams, certificates and histories; deleting a plan cascades its visits and their prescriptions. No code path hard-deletes a patient. The three hard deletes in the dental module are guarded by row locks and refusals when a visit was given, billed or has a prescription or image (REG-DENTAL tests).
  • Idempotency and uniqueness: patient card number unique per clinic and allocated by an atomic counter; receipt numbers allocated by an atomic counter inside the settle transaction (no gaps on rollback); one reversal per charge; one deposit per plan; one charge per visit; appointments protected by an exclusion constraint.

Rules found, ambiguities, and decisions needed

  • Total bill = sum of the visits' fees plus extras plus a deposit. The plan's stored total is recomputed from the rows after every add, remove, reprice and cancel.
  • Outstanding = billed and unpaid, plus treatment given and not yet billed (dental statement); upcoming visits are shown separately and not counted as owed.
  • Needs confirmation: whether a receipt may ever cover two patients (AUD-15); whether a zero-amount "nothing to refund" row should exist, because the unique reversal index means it blocks any later real refund of that charge.
