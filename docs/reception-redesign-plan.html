Reception, redesigned: implementation plan

A plan to rebuild the front-desk page so a receptionist can see everything that matters about the patient in front of them, take the right decision in one place, and hand the patient on to the cashier and then the doctor without anything falling through. Written from the current code, the live clinics' data and your requests. Nothing here is built yet: this is for your approval.

In one paragraph. Today the page tells the desk that a patient "owes 2,000" and sends them elsewhere to find out why. The redesign turns the right-hand side into a patient card that answers, on one screen: who is this, are they expected today, did they miss visits, when is their next visit, is a treatment session planned for today, and what unpaid bills do they have (itemised, tickable). The receptionist chooses the department and dentist once, then presses one button: check in and send to the cashier (or collect at the desk if allowed). The patient waits at the cashier until the ticked bills are paid, then goes to the dentist the receptionist chose, automatically. No new libraries are needed; everything is built on what the dashboard already uses.

The patient card, section by section

  1. Header and alert chips. Name, card number, age, sex, tap-to-call phone. Chips for what needs the receptionist's attention: owes money, missed visits, free follow-up, unbilled treatment, here already, no phone on file.
  2. Today. The booking (time, dentist, reason, how late), the treatment session planned for today ("Root canal, visit 2 of 3"), and why the registration fee is or is not charged.
  3. Earlier unpaid bills. Itemised, tickable, with a total. This is the heart of your request.
  4. Visits. Missed visits in the last 90 days, and the next planned visit or treatment session. A missed visit offers "call" and "rebook" (rebook opens the existing booking dialog).
  5. Send to. Department and dentist, already chosen where possible (the booking's, or the patient's usual dentist). Emergency switch and an optional "reason for visit".
  6. Action bar. The total to pay now and one primary button. The label says what will happen: "Send to cashier · then to Dr Yonas".

Phone rules, written down so they are tested

  • Thumb reach: the primary button lives in the sticky bar at the bottom, never at the top of a long card. Destructive or rare actions (waive, pay later) are in a "More" menu, not beside the main button.
  • Touch targets: at least 44 px tall. Tick boxes are the whole row, not the small square.
  • Keyboards: search opens the normal keyboard; phone fields the number pad; the payment reference the letters-and-digits keyboard with auto-capitalise off and no autocorrect; amounts the decimal pad.
  • No horizontal scroll at 320 px (the smallest phone we support). Long names and bill descriptions wrap or truncate with the full text one tap away.
  • The sheet cannot lose work: accidental swipe-down asks "Discard your selection?" only if bills are ticked or a reference is typed.
  • Slow or dropped connection: the button shows "Sending…" and stays disabled; if the connection fails the sheet stays open with the ticks and the reference intact and a plain message ("Not sent. Check the connection and press again; pressing again is safe"). Pressing twice never charges twice (the same safe-to-repeat rule as the server).
  • Landscape and rotation: rotating mid-entry keeps everything typed.
  • Dark mode and large-text settings are respected; nothing relies on colour alone.

4. The cashier and doctor handoff, as you described it

Receptionist ticks the bills the patient agrees to pay→ Chooses department and dentist→ Send to cashier→ Cashier sees the patient with those bills highlighted and collects→ Patient joins that dentist's queue automatically
  • The patient does not reach the dentist before paying. This is how today's registration fee already works (the patient is queued only once the fee is settled). The redesign extends the same gate to the bills the patient agreed to pay.
  • The cashier sees why. The worklist shows the bills as "Patient agreed to pay today (selected at reception by Hana)", pre-ticked, and says which dentist the patient goes to afterwards.
  • If the patient pays only some, or cannot pay today, the cashier can use the existing Pay later (with a reason). That lets the patient through while the debt stays on record.
  • If the patient changes their mind at the desk before pressing Send, they simply untick. After sending, only the cashier can release them.
  • A desk that is allowed to collect (receptionist who is also cashier) gets a second button, Collect here, which takes the payment and queues the patient in the same step.

The method, step by step

  1. Method. The list is the clinic's own payment accounts (set in Settings → Payments), each with its account number so the receptionist picks the right one. The first account the clinic set up is pre-selected; for a clinic that is paid mostly by Telebirr, "cash" is not the default. The last method the desk used is remembered for the next patient.
  2. Generic accounts are flagged. If a clinic has a plain "Bank" or "Mobile money" account with no bank named, an amber warning says so, with a link to name it (needs the clinic-manage permission), because an unnamed bank cannot be checked.
  3. Reference. Shown only for mobile-money and bank methods, never for cash. The hint under the box says what that provider's reference looks like (Telebirr, M-Pesa, CBE Birr, Kaafi, CBE, Abyssinia, Dashen, Awash, Siinqee). If the method needs a reference and the box is empty, the Collect button stays off and says why.
  4. Check with the bank (only when the clinic has bank verification switched on and the method names a provider). The result is one of the five states below.
  5. One method per receipt. A receipt is paid by one method. A patient paying part cash and part transfer is two separate collections, each with its own receipt, so the books and the bank agree line by line.
  6. Collect. One press: the payment, the receipt number, and (from the desk) the queue entry for the chosen dentist, all together or not at all.

After collecting

  • The receipt number appears at once (RCP-004217), with Print and "Send by SMS".
  • The patient is shown in the chosen dentist's queue with their position.
  • The card refreshes: paid bills disappear from "Earlier unpaid", the day's "To settle" count drops, and Finance's receivables move by the same amount (one definition of "owed" everywhere).
  • If the dentist has no free slot, the patient is still queued; the message says who is ahead.

8. How it is built

Frontend

  • No new libraries. What is already installed is enough: Radix (tabs, dialog, popover, scroll area, tooltip), cmdk for the keyboard-driven search, React Query for data and prefetching (hovering a row warms its card), @number-flow/react for the animated total (as on the dashboard), lucide icons, sonner toasts, react-hook-form + zod for Register & receive, Tailwind.
  • Components (the page is now one 700-line file; it becomes small pieces, each tested alone): DayStats, PatientSearch, ArrivalList, PatientCard with CardHeader, TodaySection, UnpaidBills, VisitsSection, RouteSection, ActionBar, and RegisterAndReceive.
  • Mobile and tablet: designed in section 3; the card is a bottom sheet below 640 px (fullOnMobile), two panes from 640 px, the action bar sticky, inputs 16 px, targets 44 px.
  • Payment: the existing PaymentRailPicker is reused as is, so the desk and the cashier cannot drift apart.
  • Dates: every date shows the Gregorian date with the Ethiopian date beside it (the existing helper), on the clinic's Addis day.
  • Accessibility: everything reachable by keyboard; state is never colour alone (chips carry words); focus returns to the search after a patient is received.

9. Functional testing plan

Every phase ships with its tests, and the full run (server tests, screen tests with coverage minimums, browser journeys) must be green before it is deployed, as with every fix so far.

LayerWhat is proved
Server (API) tests
  • The card: each section returned correctly for a patient with every combination (booked, treatment today, missed, next visit, unpaid, none).
  • Missed visits: only the last 90 days, only no-shows and missed sessions, never another patient's or clinic's.
  • Itemised bills match Finance's "owed" total exactly; deferred bills flagged; unbilled treatment kept apart.
  • Receive: one result whether pressed once or twice; nothing recorded if any step fails.
  • Ticked bills gate the patient: not queued until all are settled or deferred; then queued to the chosen dentist, once.
  • Bank transfers: a total above the confirmed amount refused, nothing marked paid.
  • Permissions: with and without collect rights; another clinic's patient and bills refused.
  • The Addis day around midnight for "today", "missed", "next".
  • Payment at the desk: each method; reference required only where it should be; rejected blocks; manager override recorded with name; pending refuses; an amount above the bank-confirmed one refused; one reference cannot pay twice.
  • Correct payment details (if approved): only same day, only with permission, amount never changes, audit row written, re-check runs.
Screen tests
  • Card sections render for each case, and empty states read well (no missed visits, nothing owed).
  • Tick boxes: none ticked by default, Select all, the total and the button label update, deselect works.
  • Both exits (send to cashier, collect here), the refused-payment path, and the desk without collect rights.
  • Search: best match first, keyboard use, no match leads to Register & receive.
  • Defaults: single department and usual dentist pre-selected, and changeable.
  • Phone layout at 320, 375 and 414 px and tablet portrait and landscape: sheet opens, sticky action bar, nothing clipped, no sideways scroll, 44 px targets.
  • Payment step: the five bank-check states render the right words and the right buttons; cash change due calculates; the unnamed-bank warning appears; keyboard type per field.
  • Empty screens: each of the eight in the table renders its message and next action, search is focused, "Register "Abeb"" carries the typed name into the form, and empty is never shown during loading or after a failure.
  • Failure states: each row of the states table in section 7 has a test.
  • A parity test per row of the checklist in section 7, migrated from the current page's tests.
Browser journeys (real browser, real server)
  1. A booked patient with an old bill: tick it, send to the cashier, the cashier collects, the patient appears in the chosen dentist's queue.
  2. A walk-in found by search, registration paid, one tap to check in.
  3. A new patient: Register & receive.
  4. A patient whose old bills are declined: unticked, checked in without them.
  5. A transfer paid by Telebirr: reference typed, bank check verified, collected, receipt shown, patient queued.
  6. A rejected transfer: Collect is off, manager override works, the override is visible in the audit trail.
  7. The same journeys at phone width and on a tablet.
Safety checksRepeat presses, a connection dropped mid-action, two desks on one patient, a clinic's data never visible to another, and the page staying fast with a full day's list.

11. Risks, and how each is handled

  • Queue gating is money-sensitive. Changing which bills hold a patient at the cashier must never let someone skip payment, nor leave a paid patient stuck. It is covered by server tests for both directions before anything is shown on screen, and phase 2 is deployed and checked on a test clinic first.
  • Performance. The day lists already recompute a heavy amount every 30 seconds. The card is a single patient's data, so it is light; the list query is measured and slimmed in phase 5, and in phase 1 the card is not part of the polling.
